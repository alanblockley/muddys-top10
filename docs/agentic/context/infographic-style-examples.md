# Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10
# Editorial Style Guide — Weekly Chart Infographic Copy

> **Purpose**
>
> This document defines the editorial style used for the weekly **Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10** infographic. It covers only the written copy that appears on the graphic itself. Visual layout, typography and branding are documented separately.
>
> The infographic should tell the story of the week's chart in a matter of seconds. Someone scrolling Facebook or Discord should immediately understand what happened this week and be encouraged to listen to the live countdown.

---

# Editorial Philosophy

The infographic is **not** a miniature playlist.

It is **not** a data dump.

It is **not** simply an advert for the show.

It is a visual summary of the week's biggest chart story.

Every piece of copy should answer one question:

> **"Why is this week's chart interesting?"**

The chart itself provides the excitement.

The copy simply helps tell that story.

---

# Overall Tone

The infographic should feel:

- Confident
- Conversational
- Broadcast-inspired
- Community focused
- Clean
- Modern

It should **not** feel:

- Clickbait
- Tabloid
- Overly corporate
- Hyperbolic
- AI generated

---

# 1. Headline

## Purpose

Immediately communicate what the viewer is looking at.

The headline should identify the chart first.

The weekly story belongs elsewhere.

---

## Preferred Structure

Always lead with the show.

Examples:

> Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10

> This Week's Top 10

> Muddy's Weekly Top 10

---

## Optional Secondary Hook

If space allows, include a short editorial hook.

Examples:

- A New Number One
- The Battle Continues
- A Chart Full of Surprises
- A Week of Big Movers
- Another Close Finish

These should never replace the primary headline.

---

## Character Limit

Primary headline:

25–40 characters

Secondary hook:

15–35 characters

---

## Good Examples

### ✓

Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10

Simple.

Recognisable.

Timeless.

---

### ✓

Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10

**A New Number One**

Creates curiosity without giving away details.

---

### ✓

This Week's Top 10

Clean.

Works when branding already exists elsewhere.

---

### ✓

Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10

**The Battle Continues**

Suggests ongoing chart movement.

---

## Examples to Reject

### ✗

YOU WON'T BELIEVE THIS WEEK'S CHART

Too clickbait.

---

### ✗

The Greatest Chart Ever

Meaningless hype.

---

### ✗

Week Ending 26 July 2026

Useful metadata.

Poor headline.

---

# 2. Subhead / Tagline

## Purpose

Reinforce ownership of the chart.

The tagline should rarely change.

It has become part of the show's identity.

---

## Canonical Tagline

> **Your requests. Your music. Your chart.**

This is the preferred tagline.

Use consistently.

---

## Alternative (Occasional)

Compiled from songs played by our DJs and your requests.

Suitable for educational versions.

Not as strong for weekly graphics.

---

## Character Limit

25–45 characters.

---

# 3. Chart Story

## Purpose

Summarise the week's biggest narrative in one or two sentences.

This is editorial copy.

It should explain the significance of the week's movement.

---

## Length

20–45 words.

Maximum:

60 words.

---

## Good Structure

Story first.

Statistics second.

Instead of:

> Artist X moves from #4 to #1.

Prefer:

> After weeks of building momentum, Artist X finally claims the number one spot.

---

## Examples

### ✓

After spending weeks knocking on the door, this week's biggest climber finally claims the number one spot.

---

### ✓

A brand new entry shakes up the Top 10, while last week's champion refuses to disappear.

---

### ✓

Another incredibly close week sees the top positions separated by the narrowest of margins.

---

### ✓

Consistency wins again, with familiar favourites holding firm while new challengers begin to emerge.

---

### ✓

One artist dominates this week's chart with multiple entries, but the race for number one is far from over.

---

## Reject

### ✗

There were 43 songs played.

No story.

---

### ✗

Congratulations to everyone.

No information.

---

### ✗

This week's chart is awesome!!

Meaningless.

---

### ✗

This week's chart contains ten songs.

Obviously.

---

# Canon Pattern

Chart stories should answer:

Why should someone care about this week's chart?

Not:

What happened numerically?

---

# 4. Movement Badges

## Philosophy

Badges should be understood in less than half a second.

Short.

Consistent.

Visual.

---

## New Entry

Preferred:

**NEW**

Alternative:

DEBUT

Avoid:

NEW ENTRY

Too long.

---

## Climber

Preferred:

▲ +3

Alternative:

+3

Avoid:

UP THREE PLACES

---

## Faller

Preferred:

▼ -2

Alternative:

-2

Avoid:

DOWN TWO PLACES

---

## Non-Mover

Preferred:

=

Alternative:

HOLDS

Avoid:

NON-MOVER

---

## Re-entry

Preferred:

RETURNS

Alternative:

RE-ENTRY

Avoid:

BACK AGAIN

---

## Badge Rules

Never exceed:

8 characters.

---

# 5. Statistics Section

## Purpose

Support the story.

Not overwhelm it.

Readers should understand the week's activity at a glance.

---

## Recommended Statistics

Number of new entries

Biggest climber

Non-movers

Songs moving up

Songs moving down

Optional:

Total unique tracks played

---

## Preferred Labels

NEW ENTRIES

BIGGEST CLIMBER

MOVING UP

MOVING DOWN

HOLDS

---

Avoid:

Statistics

Weekly Data

Chart Metrics

---

## Tone

Data supports narrative.

Narrative always comes first.

# 7. Things That Look Wrong

## Too Wordy

Avoid paragraphs.

The infographic should be readable within ten seconds.

---

## Too Formal

Avoid:

"The following statistical analysis demonstrates..."

---

## Too Corporate

Avoid:

"Our valued customers..."

---

## Too Radio DJ

Avoid:

"LET'S GOOOOOOOO!"

---

## Too Clickbait

Avoid:

"This changes EVERYTHING!"

---

## Too Generic

Avoid:

"Another great week."

Great.

Why?

---

## Story Text

Sentence case.

Natural punctuation.

---

# Punctuation Rules

Use:

Full stops.

Commas.

Ellipses sparingly.

Avoid:

Exclamation marks unless genuinely earned.

Never use multiple punctuation.

Never:

!!!

???

---

# Numbers

Use numerals.

Good:

Top 10

3 weeks

2am SLT

Bad:

Three Weeks

Ten Songs

# Overall Editorial Voice

Every infographic should feel like a snapshot taken halfway through a radio show.

It should make someone think:

> "I wonder what happened this week."

not

> "Someone tried very hard to advertise something."

The copy should always reinforce that the Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10 is a living community chart shaped by the music played throughout the week and the requests of its listeners.

The chart tells the story.

The copy simply helps people see it.
