# DJ Toohey's Top 10 — Golden Radio Reads
## Canonical Style Reference (v1.0)

> **Purpose**
>
> This document captures the canonical style, tone and rhythm of the weekly **Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10** countdown, presented by **DJ Toohey** with co-host **JP**.
>
> These examples are not intended to be copied verbatim. They exist to teach an AI how this show sounds, how it tells the story of the chart, and how each type of radio read should feel.
>
> The chart is compiled from songs played throughout the week at Muddy's Music Cafe and shaped by listener requests. The presenters guide the audience through the story of that week's chart rather than simply reading a list of songs.

---

# 1. Show Intro

## Purpose

Sets the atmosphere for the countdown, welcomes both inworld and stream listeners, reminds the audience that the chart belongs to the community, and builds anticipation without giving anything away.

Target:
- 80–130 words
- 35–60 seconds

---

## Example A

Good morning everyone and welcome along to another Saturday here at Muddy's Music Cafe. I'm DJ Toohey, JP's alongside me as always, and after another week of requests, live sets and plenty of great music, we've got another Top 10 ready to count down.

There's been movement right across this week's chart. One artist has continued an incredible run, we've got a brand new entry making its first appearance, and the battle for number one... well... it's a lot closer than you might think.

So wherever you're listening, inworld or on the stream, settle in. This is your music, your requests and your weekly countdown.

### Why it works

- Welcomes everyone naturally.
- Immediately reminds listeners how the chart exists.
- Creates curiosity instead of revealing facts.
- Ends by handing ownership back to the audience.

Approx.
- 105 words
- ~45 seconds

---

## Example B

Saturday mornings mean one thing around here... it's time to find out what you've been listening to all week.

Welcome to another Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10. I'm DJ Toohey, JP's keeping me honest beside me, and over the next half hour we'll work our way through the songs that have been making the biggest impact at Muddy's over the last seven days.

There are a few surprises waiting this week, a couple of familiar names refusing to go away, and one track that's making a serious case for being this week's story.

Let's find out where everything landed.

### Why it works

- Opens with a familiar tradition.
- Creates story rather than hype.
- Uses understated language.
- Natural broadcast cadence.

Approx.
- 100 words
- ~40 seconds

---

# Canon Pattern

Every intro should:

- Welcome first.
- Explain why we're here.
- Mention JP naturally.
- Remind listeners this is their chart.
- Hint at the week's story.
- Never spoil the outcome.

# 2. Top 10 Intro

## Purpose

Marks the transition from the general show into the countdown itself.

Target:
40–70 words

---

## Example A

Right then... enough talking about it.

Ten songs remain.

Some are exactly where you'd expect.

Some absolutely aren't.

Let's head into this week's Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10 and see what you've created.

### Why it works

Very conversational.

Builds anticipation with short sentences.

---

## Example B

We've reached the moment everyone's been waiting for.

The requests have been counted.

The plays have been tallied.

Now there's only one thing left to do...

Let's count down this week's Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10.

### Why it works

Feels ceremonial without sounding over-produced.

Approx.
55 words

---

# Pattern

The Top 10 Intro should feel like opening the curtain.

Never explain the chart again.

Simply signal:

"It's time."

---

# 3. Position Read (Lower Chart — #10 to #6)

## Purpose

Introduce one position.

These reads should be light, conversational and informative.

Target:
25–45 words

---

## Example A

Starting us off at number ten, this one's held on for another week. It may not be climbing, but it's proving it's got staying power, and around here that's never a bad thing.

---

### Why it works

Focuses on the chart story.

Doesn't overstate the achievement.

---

## Example B

Into number eight this week, and this one's quietly been building momentum over the past couple of weekends. Every request has helped keep it right in the mix.

---

### Why it works

Acknowledges listener influence.

Feels community-focused.

---

## Example C

Number seven belongs to a track that's been hovering around this part of the chart for a while now. Not making headlines... just refusing to disappear.

---

### Why it works

Dry humour.

Story over statistics.

---

# Pattern

Lower-chart reads:

- establish context
- acknowledge movement
- avoid drama
- feel relaxed

---

# 4. Position Read (Mid Chart — #5–#4)

## Purpose

Energy begins increasing.

Audience starts anticipating the finish.

Target:
35–60 words

---

## Example A

We've reached the business end now.

Number five marks the halfway point between the chasing pack and the podium, and this week's occupant has earned every bit of its place after another strong week of requests.

---

## Example B

Into fourth this week... and this is where it starts getting interesting.

So close to the top three, yet just missing out. Still, it's another excellent week for a track that's become a real favourite around Muddy's.

---

# Pattern

Energy rises naturally.

Don't manufacture excitement.

The chart creates it.

---

# 5. Position Read (Top Three)

## Purpose

Maximum anticipation.

Slow the pace.

Allow pauses.

Target:
45–70 words

---

## Example A

Only three songs remain.

Three tracks that have dominated the conversation all week.

By now every play matters.

Every request matters.

And every position feels earned.

Let's begin with this week's number three...

---

## Example B

If you've been trying to guess this week's winner, this is where things become very interesting.

Number three is a fantastic result... but it also means two songs have done just a little bit better.

---

# Pattern

Use shorter sentences.

Increase pauses.

Let silence build tension.

Never rush.

---

# Pattern Notes (Part 1)

Across every read above, several characteristics consistently define the show's identity.

## Story Before Statistics

Movement is explained before numbers.

Instead of:

> Up three places.

Prefer:

> It's quietly been building momentum over the past few weekends.

---

## Understatement

Avoid:

- unbelievable
- incredible
- explosive
- massive
- phenomenal

Prefer:

- quietly building
- refusing to disappear
- making a case
- staying power
- another strong week

---

## Community Ownership

Frequently reinforce:

- your requests
- our DJs
- our chart
- around Muddy's
- this community

The chart belongs to the listeners.

---

## Build Rather Than Announce

Never rush reveals.

Good reads create anticipation naturally before giving the position.

---

## Conversational Broadcast Rhythm

Reads should sound spoken, not written.

Use:

- sentence fragments
- short pauses
- occasional understatement

Avoid perfectly balanced prose or "AI-polished" language.

---

# 6. Position Read (New Entry)

## Purpose

Celebrate a song making its first appearance in the chart. The focus isn't just on the debut itself, but on the story behind how it earned its place. A new entry should feel like a fresh chapter in this week's countdown.

Target:
- 35–60 words
- 15–25 seconds

---

## Example A

Every chart needs a fresh face, and this week we've got exactly that.

Making its debut inside the Muddy's Top 10, this one's been gathering momentum over the past week and has finally broken through. The first appearance is always special... now the question becomes how long it can stick around.

### Why it works

- Celebrates without exaggeration.
- Looks ahead rather than simply announcing the debut.
- Leaves listeners curious about its future.

Approx.
- 65 words
- ~25 seconds

---

## Example B

It's always nice seeing a new name arrive in the countdown.

This week's highest new entry has turned plenty of heads over the last few sets, and thanks to your requests it's earned its place among this week's Top 10.

Welcome to the chart.

### Why it works

- Gives credit to listeners.
- Feels warm rather than dramatic.
- Short and easy to deliver.

Approx.
- 55 words
- ~20 seconds

---

## Example C

Debuting this week, and proving that good songs don't always need weeks to get noticed.

A strong few days, plenty of requests, and now a place in this week's countdown.

Let's see where the journey goes from here.

### Why it works

- Creates an ongoing narrative.
- Sounds optimistic without overselling.

---

# Canon Pattern

A new entry isn't the destination.

It's the beginning of a story.

---

# 7. Position Read (Biggest Climber)

## Purpose

Celebrate significant upward movement.

The emphasis should be on momentum, not simply arithmetic.

Target:
40–70 words

---

## Example A

This week's biggest climber has been impossible to ignore.

Seven days ago it was sitting comfortably in the bottom half of the chart.

Now...

It's knocking on the door of the very top.

That's what a great week of requests can do.

### Why it works

- Focuses on progression.
- Uses pauses naturally.
- Credits the community.

---

## Example B

Some songs creep up the chart.

This one didn't.

Week after week it's been building support, and this week's climb is thoroughly deserved.

The biggest mover in this week's Top 10.

### Why it works

- Contrast creates rhythm.
- Doesn't rely on exact numbers.

---

## Example C

Momentum matters.

And no track found more of it this week than this one.

A huge jump up the countdown and suddenly it's become one of the genuine stories of this week's chart.

### Why it works

- Tells the story.
- Avoids shouting.

---

# Canon Pattern

Avoid saying:

> Up five places!

Instead explain:

> It's become one of the stories of the week.

---

# 8. Top Five Recap

## Purpose

Reset the audience before entering the business end of the chart.

Target:
40–70 words

---

## Example A

Let's quickly catch our breath.

So far we've counted down numbers ten through five.

We've welcomed a brand new entry, seen some familiar favourites holding firm, and watched one of this week's biggest movers make its presence felt.

Only four songs remain...

### Why it works

- Summarises stories.
- Doesn't simply repeat titles.

Approx.
- 60 words

---

## Example B

That's the bottom half of this week's chart done and dusted.

If you've missed any of it, don't worry, there's still plenty to come.

Four songs left.

One number one.

And it's still anyone's guess.

### Why it works

- Builds anticipation.
- Short broadcast rhythm.

---

# Pattern

The recap should remind listeners what happened...

...not read the chart again.

---

# 9. Top Three Recap

Maximum tension.

Target:
30–60 words

---

Number three has fallen.

Number two is waiting.

Which means there's only one question left.

What is this week's number one?

### Why it works

- Extremely simple.
- Lets silence create drama.

---

## Example B

We've reached the moment the whole countdown has been building towards.

Only two songs remain.

Both have had fantastic weeks.

But only one can finish on top.

### Why it works

- Calm confidence.
- Doesn't overhype.

---

## Example C

Every request.

Every play.

Every set this week...

Has led us here.

Two songs remain.

One chart topper.

Let's finish the countdown.

### Why it works

- Signature rhythm.
- Feels cinematic without becoming theatrical.

---

# Pattern

Slow down.

Don't fill every second.

Silence is part of the read.

Deliver the payoff.

The reveal should feel earned rather than explosive.

Target:
50–90 words

After another fantastic week at Muddy's Music Cafe...

After every request...

Every DJ set...

Every movement throughout the countdown...

There's only one song left.

Congratulations to this week's number one.

You've earned it.

### Why it works

- Includes the whole community.
- Understated finish.

Approx.
- 70 words
- ~30 seconds

---

## Example B

It stayed in the conversation all week.

It kept showing up in requests.

It kept finding another play.

And this week...

It finishes exactly where every artist wants to be.

Your Muddy's Music Cafe number one.

### Why it works

- Story before position.
- Natural reveal.

---

## Example C

Some weeks the winner is obvious.

This wasn't one of them.

But after another incredibly close battle...

One song has done just enough.

Congratulations to this week's Muddy's Music Cafe number one.

### Why it works

- References the week's story.
- Doesn't sound manufactured.

---

# Canon Pattern

Never shout the winner.

Keep those requests coming, keep supporting your favourites, and we'll be back to tell the story all over again.

- Reinforces the chart cycle.
- Leaves listeners feeling involved.

# Complete Pattern Notes

## The Chart Is the Story

The presenter doesn't create excitement.

The chart does.

Every read should answer:

> **Why is this position interesting?**

rather than:

> **What position is this?**

---

## Story Before Statistics

Avoid:

> Up three places.

Prefer:

> Quietly building momentum over the past few weekends.

---

## Understatement Wins

Avoid commercial radio language.

Instead use phrases like:

- quietly building
- finding momentum
- staying power
- another strong week
- making a case
- refusing to disappear
- one of this week's stories
- earned its place
- knocking on the door
- just enough

---

## Community Ownership

Frequently reinforce:

- your requests
- our DJs
- around Muddy's
- this community
- our chart

The audience should always feel they helped create the countdown.

---

## Sentence Rhythm

Mix:

- long setup
- short payoff

Example:

> It's been building support for weeks.
>
> This week...
>
> It arrives.

---

## Controlled Energy Curve

The emotional energy should rise naturally throughout the countdown:

| Section | Energy |
|---------|--------|
| Show Intro | Warm |
| Top 10 Intro | Anticipation |
| 10–6 | Conversational |
| 5–4 | Building |
| Top 3 | Deliberate tension |
| #1 | Satisfying payoff |
| Outro | Warm reflection |

Never jump straight to maximum excitement.

---

## Repetition Is a Tool

Strategic repetition adds emphasis.

For example:

> Every request.
>
> Every play.
>
> Every set.
>
> Has led us here.

Used sparingly, this creates rhythm without sounding artificial.

---

## Things DJ Toohey Doesn't Say

Avoid:

- "What an absolute banger!"
- "This one is on fire!"
- "An incredible smash hit!"
- "Let's crank it up!"
- "This is absolutely insane!"
- "You won't believe this!"

These sound like generic commercial radio.

---

## What Makes This Show Different

The Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10 isn't simply a countdown.

It's a weekly story told through music.

The presenters are guides, not hype men.

They celebrate movement without exaggeration, acknowledge the community at every opportunity, and allow the chart itself to provide the excitement.

The listener should always leave feeling that they didn't just hear a list of songs.

They experienced the story of a week's worth of music, requests and shared moments at Muddy's Music Cafe.
