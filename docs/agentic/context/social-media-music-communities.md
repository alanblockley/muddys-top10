# AI Reference: Social Media Posts for Weekly Music Chart Shows

> **Purpose**
>
> This document defines practical writing patterns for an AI agent creating social media posts around a weekly music chart countdown in a close-knit online music community.
>
> The audience is not a passive mass-market radio audience. It is a community of listeners, DJs, regular requesters, venue members and music fans who may recognise one another, influence the chart and care about the weekly result.
>
> The goal is to make posts feel current, social and community-owned without sounding corporate, spammy or artificially overexcited.

---

# 1. What Drives Engagement in Music Communities

## Chart Reveals Create Natural Anticipation

A countdown already contains a built-in question:

> What made number one?

That question creates anticipation without needing exaggerated marketing language.

Useful pre-show framing:

> Three new entries, one huge climber and a very close race at the top.

Useful post-show framing:

> The result is in — and this week's biggest move came from a track nobody expected to return so strongly.

The post should reveal enough to create interest while preserving the main payoff when the show has not yet aired.

---

## The “Did You See?” Effect

Community posts work when they make the chart feel like something people are already discussing.

Examples:

> Did you see what jumped back into the Top 10 this week?

> That number-two result was much closer than it looks.

> One artist has managed to place two tracks in the same countdown.

This creates a mild sense that the reader may have missed a notable moment.

Do not manufacture false urgency. The story must be genuinely supported by the chart.

---

## Community Pride

Listeners become more invested when they feel their requests helped shape the result.

Good phrasing:

> Your requests helped push this one into the Top Five.

> A strong week of requests has brought this track back into the chart.

> One of the community favourites has finally reached a new peak.

This turns the chart from a passive ranking into evidence of collective participation.

Avoid implying that one named listener single-handedly caused a chart result unless that is factually known and appropriate to share.

---

## Debate and Discussion

Chart results naturally invite opinions.

Useful questions:

> Do you agree with this week's number one?

> Which track should be higher?

> What deserves a place in next week's Top 10?

> Which movement surprised you most?

> Is this returning classic still deserving of a Top 10 place?

The question should be easy to answer without requiring a long explanation.

Avoid vague prompts such as:

> Thoughts?

A specific question produces better responses.

---

## Nostalgia

Returning tracks and older songs often trigger stronger emotional responses than routine chart movement.

Useful framing:

> A familiar favourite is back in the Top 10.

> This one has found its way back onto the chart years after its original release.

> A classic has returned — and clearly the community still has plenty of love for it.

Nostalgia works best when attached to a recognisable memory, era or shared listening experience.

Do not overstate every older song as “iconic” or “legendary.”

---

## Personal Connection Between Requests and Results

The core relationship is:

```text
Listener request
→ DJ play
→ weekly activity
→ chart result
```

The social post should reinforce that loop.

Examples:

> Every request helps shape next week's chart.

> Heard something you want to see climb? Keep requesting it.

> The songs played and requested throughout the week become Saturday's countdown.

This gives the audience a clear reason to participate again.

# 2. Post Structure That Works

A strong chart post usually follows this sequence:

```text
Hook
↓
Chart highlights
↓
Community framing
↓
Question or CTA
↓
Essential schedule details
↓
Limited hashtags
```

---

## Hook Line

The first one or two sentences must carry the strongest story because feed previews may cut off everything after them.

Good hooks:

> The latest Top 10 has landed — and this week's biggest climber moved an incredible 18 places.

> A new number one, two returning favourites and one very unexpected Top Five entry.

> This week's chart was anything but predictable.

Weak hooks:

> Hello everyone, we hope you're having a lovely week.

> Here is another post about our weekly chart.

> We are delighted to announce the release of this week's rankings.

Start with what changed.

---

## Information Payload

Include only the strongest two to four points.

Useful content:

* number one
* biggest climber
* new entries
* artist with multiple tracks
* major fall
* return or re-entry
* new peak
* unusually close result

Example:

> Ella Langley holds the top spot, while The Weeknd delivers the week's biggest climb. Taylor Swift moves into the Top Two, and one returning classic makes its way back into the countdown.

Do not reproduce all ten positions in the caption when the infographic already shows them.

---

## Engagement Driver

End the main body with one clear invitation.

Examples:

> Which track should be number one next week?

> What surprised you most in this week's chart?

> Did your request make the Top 10?

> Which song are you backing to climb next Saturday?

One strong question is better than four weak questions.

---

## Hashtag Usage

Use approximately:

```text
Facebook: 3–6 hashtags
Discord: 0–3 hashtags
Short-form teaser: 2–5 hashtags
```

Useful categories:

* venue or show name
* community or platform
* music chart
* live DJ
* internet radio

Example:

```text
#MuddysMusicCafe #Top10 #SecondLifeMusic #LiveDJ
```

Avoid:

* long blocks of 15–30 hashtags
* irrelevant trending tags
* repeating near-identical tags
* tags that make a community post look like advertising spam

---

## Post Length

### Facebook

Recommended:

```text
100–250 words
```

Shorter posts can work for a simple chart card, but community groups often respond well to a little narrative context.

### Discord

Recommended:

```text
40–120 words
```

Use short paragraphs and get to the point quickly.

### Teasers

Recommended:

```text
25–80 words
```

Teasers should create curiosity rather than summarise the whole chart.

---

## Emoji Use

Use emoji as signposts.

Good:

```text
👑 Number one
📈 Climber
📉 Faller
🔥 Hot entry
🎵 Request prompt
```

Use approximately three to six relevant emoji in a Facebook post and fewer in Discord.

Bad:

```text
🎉🔥🎵💜✨🎤🚀👑💥🎶
```

Emoji should clarify structure, not decorate every sentence.

# 3. Platform-Specific Conventions

## Facebook

### Use Image and Text Together

The infographic supplies the data.

The caption supplies:

* the story
* the community angle
* the question
* the schedule

Do not repeat every visible chart row in prose.

---

### Longer Community Posts

Facebook groups allow slightly more context than fast-moving platforms.

A good structure:

```text
Hook paragraph

Two short chart-highlight paragraphs

Community or listener acknowledgement

Question and schedule

Hashtags
```

Use line breaks every one to three sentences.

Large unbroken paragraphs are difficult to scan on mobile.

---

### Questions Drive Comments

Good Facebook questions:

> Which track deserves a bigger climb next week?

> Are you surprised by the new number one?

> Which returning song made you smile?

Avoid questions requiring specialist knowledge.

---

### Tagging Etiquette

Tag only when:

* the person or organisation is directly relevant;
* tagging is normal in that community;
* the account is genuine and likely to welcome the mention.

Do not tag random artist accounts simply because their song appears in the chart.

Do not mass-tag DJs, listeners or venue staff to force reach.

---

### Facebook Example

```markdown
🎶 **The latest Top 10 has landed!**

This week's chart brings a familiar face back to number one, while one track delivers the biggest climb of the week and a returning favourite finds its way back into the countdown.

Your requests and the music played by our DJs throughout the week helped shape every position.

Which track should be climbing higher next Saturday?

Catch the full countdown live every Saturday at 2am SLT.

#MuddysMusicCafe #Top10 #SecondLifeMusic #LiveDJ
```

---

## Discord

### Short and Immediate

Discord messages should sound like someone sharing news with the room.

Good:

> The new Top 10 is here 👀
> We have a retained #1, a massive climber and one classic back in the chart.
> Which result surprised you most?

Avoid long formal introductions.

---

### Embed-Friendly Links

Place links on their own line where possible.

Example:

```text
Full chart:
<link>
```

Do not bury the link inside a long paragraph.

---

### Spoiler Tags

Use spoiler formatting when:

* the countdown has not yet aired;
* the channel expects spoiler protection;
* the number-one reveal is a meaningful event.

Example:

```text
This week's #1 is ||Artist – Track||.
```

Do not use spoiler tags after the official chart has already been published unless the community prefers them.

---

### Reaction Prompts

Useful:

> React with 🎵 if your favourite made the chart.

> React with 🔥 for the biggest climber.

> React with 👑 if you agree with number one.

Use one reaction prompt rather than asking for five different reactions.

---

### Channel Tone

General channel:

> The new Top 10 is out — take a look!

Music-specific channel:

> Seven climbers this week, including a huge 18-place jump. Which movement are you backing?

Match the level of detail to the channel's purpose.

---

### Discord Example

```markdown
🎵 **This week's Top 10 is live!**

A retained #1, one huge climber and a familiar favourite back in the countdown.

Which result surprised you most?

React with 👑 if you agree with the top spot.
```

---

## Short-Form and Teaser Posts

### Build Anticipation Without Spoiling

Good teaser content:

* number of new entries
* scale of biggest movement
* close battle for number one
* returning artist
* unusual chart pattern

Avoid revealing the winner unless the teaser is meant to announce the final chart.

Examples:

> Three new entries shake up this week's countdown — and the race for number one was closer than expected.

> One track jumps more than 20 places, but does it climb high enough to take the crown?

> A returning classic, two artists with multiple entries and a battle at the top. Saturday's chart is going to be busy.

---

### “Coming This Saturday” Pattern

```markdown
Coming this Saturday…

📈 One enormous climber  
🆕 Two new entries  
👑 A serious battle for number one  

The full Top 10 countdown starts at 2am SLT.
```

# 4. Music-Specific Social Conventions

## Artist Credit

Use:

```text
Artist – Track Title
```

or:

```text
"Track Title" by Artist
```

Be consistent within the same post.

Preserve:

* official artist spelling
* featured-artist credits
* official track capitalisation where available

Do not tag unrelated or unofficial accounts.

---

## Movement Language in Social Text

Movement can be made visually scannable:

```text
🔥 Straight in at #3
📈 Climbs to a new peak
🚀 Up 12 places
📉 Slips back to #7
➡️ Holds firm at #4
🔁 Returns to the Top 10
```

Use stronger wording only for genuinely large changes.

Suggested scale:

```text
1–2 places: edges up, moves up, climbs
3–6 places: jumps, rises, pushes higher
7+ places: surges, rockets, storms
```

---

## Tell a Story, Not Just a List

Weak:

> #1 Artist A
> #2 Artist B
> #3 Artist C

Better:

> Artist A holds the crown, but Artist B is now only one place behind. Meanwhile, Artist C delivers the week's biggest climb and looks like a serious challenger for next Saturday.

The infographic can display the list. The caption should explain what it means.

---

## Chart Card Format

A chart-card post contains:

1. Infographic
2. One-line hook
3. Two or three highlights
4. One community question
5. Schedule or request CTA
6. Limited hashtags

Example:

```markdown
The latest chart card is here — and this week's biggest move came from a returning favourite.

👑 Artist A holds #1  
📈 Artist B climbs eight places  
🆕 Artist C debuts inside the Top Five  

Which track should be higher next week?

Catch the full countdown Saturday at 2am SLT.
```

---

## Spoiler Etiquette

Reveal number one when:

* the live countdown has finished;
* the chart graphic is officially published;
* the post is explicitly a results post;
* the audience expects immediate results.

Avoid revealing number one when:

* promoting the upcoming live countdown;
* the community values listening to the reveal;
* the post is labelled as a teaser;
* the show has not yet aired.

Useful distinction:

```text
Pre-show: Tease the battle.
Post-show: Celebrate the winner.
```

# 5. Community Tone vs Brand Tone

## Community Tone Comes First

A close-knit music community generally responds better to:

> You lot pushed this one back into the Top 10.

than:

> We are pleased to announce the latest results of our weekly audience engagement initiative.

The writing should still be accurate and polished, but it should sound human.

---

## Conversational, Not Careless

Good:

> That is a huge jump.

> This one refuses to leave the chart.

> We did not expect to see this back so soon.

Too corporate:

> We are delighted to present this week's high-performing chart participants.

Too sloppy:

> omg this tune went nuts lolllll

Use natural spoken language with competent grammar.

---

## Inside References

Inside references can create belonging when they are:

* widely understood in the community;
* affectionate rather than excluding;
* brief enough that newcomers can still follow.

Examples:

> You kept requesting it — so here it is again.

> Apparently none of you are ready to let this one leave the chart.

Avoid unexplained references that make new members feel locked out.

---

## Celebrate the Community

Useful phrasing:

> Your requests made this chart.

> The music played by our DJs and requested by you shaped every position.

> This is our community's weekly countdown.

> You helped push this track to a new peak.

The chart should feel shared, not imposed by the brand.

---

## Inclusive Language

Prefer:

* we
* our chart
* our countdown
* this week's community picks
* your requests
* the songs you backed

Avoid:

* customers
* consumers
* target audience
* users
* engagement cohort

---

# 6. Calls to Action That Work

## Soft CTAs

Use when the main aim is tune-in awareness.

Examples:

> Catch the full countdown Saturday at 2am SLT.

> Join us next Saturday as we do it all again.

> Hear the full Top 10 live this weekend.

---

## Engagement CTAs

Use when the goal is comments or discussion.

Examples:

> What's your pick for next week's number one?

> Which track should be higher?

> What was the biggest surprise this week?

> Which song are you requesting next?

---

## Share CTAs

Use carefully and only when there is a natural reason.

Examples:

> Share it if your request made the chart.

> Know someone who will be pleased to see this track back? Send it their way.

Avoid:

> LIKE, SHARE AND TAG THREE FRIENDS!

That feels transactional and out of place in a community setting.

---

## Facebook CTAs

Best options:

* comment with a pick
* name a track
* explain a surprise
* share when personally relevant

Example:

> Tell us which track you're backing for next week's Top 10.

---

## Discord CTAs

Best options:

* reaction
* short reply
* song nomination
* request prompt

Example:

> React with 🎵 if your favourite made it.

---

## What Not to Do

Avoid:

* “Smash that like button.”
* “Like and subscribe.”
* “Comment YES if you agree.”
* “Tag five friends.”
* repeated all-caps commands
* fake urgency
* guilt-based language
* multiple CTAs in one short post
* engagement bait unrelated to the chart

A community post should invite participation, not demand it.

# Recommended Writing Patterns

## Pre-Show Teaser

```markdown
👀 This week's Top 10 is going to be interesting.

We've got two new entries, one major return and a battle at the top that stayed close right until the final count.

No spoilers yet — catch the full countdown Saturday at 2am SLT.

Which track are you hoping made the chart?
```

---

## Post-Show Results Post

```markdown
🎶 **The latest Top 10 has landed!**

Artist A holds onto number one, while Artist B delivers the week's biggest climb and Artist C returns to the chart after several weeks away.

Your requests and the music played by our DJs helped shape every position.

Which result surprised you most — and what should be climbing next week?

Catch the countdown live every Saturday at 2am SLT.

#Top10 #LiveDJ #InternetRadio #MusicCommunity
```

---

## Quiet-Chart Post

```markdown
Not every chart needs chaos.

This week is all about staying power, with several tracks holding firm and the top end of the countdown proving difficult to break into.

Which song has earned its place — and which one needs a push next week?
```

---

## Volatile-Chart Post

```markdown
This week's chart has been turned upside down.

Seven songs climb, two new entries arrive and one track makes the biggest move we've seen in weeks.

Do you agree with the new order?
```

---

## Nostalgia-Led Post

```markdown
A familiar favourite is back in the Top 10.

It may not be new, but your requests have given it another chart run — and it clearly still means something to this community.

Which older track deserves a return next?
```

---

# AI Writing Rules

When generating weekly posts:

1. Lead with the strongest genuine chart story.
2. Mention no more than two to four highlights.
3. Connect the result to listener requests or DJ plays.
4. Use one clear question or CTA.
5. Match length and tone to the platform.
6. Use emoji as labels, not decoration.
7. Avoid repeating the entire infographic in text.
8. Preserve exact artist and track spelling.
9. Do not spoil number one in a pre-show teaser.
10. Avoid corporate language and forced enthusiasm.
11. Do not exaggerate routine movement.
12. Use “we,” “our” and “your” to reinforce community ownership.
13. Keep hashtags relevant and limited.
14. Make each week's post reflect what actually changed.
15. End with either participation, tune-in information or both.

---

# Final Quality Checklist

Before publishing, verify:

* [ ] The first two lines contain the strongest hook.
* [ ] The post matches the supplied chart data.
* [ ] Artist and track names are accurate.
* [ ] The number-one result is revealed only when appropriate.
* [ ] Movement language matches the size of the movement.
* [ ] The community's role is acknowledged.
* [ ] The post contains one clear engagement prompt.
* [ ] Emoji use is restrained.
* [ ] Hashtags are limited and relevant.
* [ ] The tone sounds conversational rather than corporate.
* [ ] The post does not merely repeat every chart position.
* [ ] Schedule details are accurate.
* [ ] The post is easy to scan on mobile.
* [ ] No random artist accounts or community members are tagged.
* [ ] The CTA feels inviting rather than desperate.

---

## Core Principle

> The best weekly chart posts do not merely announce a ranking. They make the community feel that it created the result, give people one good reason to discuss it, and make next week's chart feel worth returning for.
