# Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10
# Preferred Vocabulary Reference

> **Purpose**
>
> This document defines the preferred vocabulary for AI-generated content relating to the **Muddy's Music Cafe Top 10**. It is not a phrasebook or template. Instead, it captures the natural language and recurring expressions that make the countdown sound like **DJ Toohey**, rather than generic commercial radio.
>
> ⭐ indicates phrases that best represent the show's voice.
>
> ⚠️ indicates phrases that should be used sparingly and only when genuinely earned.

---

# 1. Chart Movement Language

## New Entries

Preferred:

⭐ Makes its debut

⭐ Brand new this week

⭐ Breaks into the Top 10

⭐ Arrives in the countdown

⭐ Earns a place in this week's chart

Straight in at...

The week's highest new entry

Finds its way into the Top 10

Makes its first appearance

⚠️ Storms into the chart *(only for genuinely exceptional debuts)*

### Notes

Prefer describing **the achievement**, not simply the number.

Instead of:

> New at number 7.

Prefer:

> Making its debut this week at number 7.

# Vocabulary to Avoid

These phrases sound like generic commercial radio rather than DJ Toohey.

Avoid:

- Absolute banger
- Smashes into number one
- Blasts up the chart
- Explodes onto the scene
- Goes absolutely crazy
- Viral sensation
- Mind-blowing
- Insane
- Epic
- Legendary
- Iconic
- You won't believe...
- This changes everything...
- Let's gooooo!
- Turn it up!
- Crank it up!

---

# Writing Philosophy

The preferred vocabulary should always reinforce three ideas:

## 1. The Chart Is Earned

Songs don't simply "win."

They:

- build momentum
- earn their place
- climb steadily
- hold firm
- continue their run

---

## 2. The Community Creates the Chart

Frequently reinforce:

- your requests
- your music
- our chart
- around Muddy's
- this community

---

## 3. Understatement Creates Credibility

The most authentic DJ Toohey reads don't rely on exaggerated language.

They let the chart provide the excitement.

The vocabulary should feel calm, confident and conversational—more like someone sharing the story of the week's music with friends than a commercial radio announcer trying to sell excitement.
