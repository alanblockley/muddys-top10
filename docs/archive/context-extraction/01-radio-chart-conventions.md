# Prompt: Radio Chart Show Conventions

**Target file:** `docs/agentic/context/radio-chart-show-conventions.md`

---

## Paste this into ChatGPT:

```
I'm building an AI system that generates content for a weekly radio chart countdown show. The AI needs foundational knowledge about how professional radio chart countdowns work — the universal conventions that any good chart show follows, regardless of the specific station or DJ.

Please write a comprehensive reference document covering:

## 1. Show Structure
- How a chart countdown is paced (10 down to 1)
- Why tension and anticipation matter
- The role of recaps (Top 10 intro, Top 5 recap, Top 3 recap)
- How the #1 reveal is handled as the climax
- Teasing ("still to come…", "will it hold on?")
- How non-movers vs climbers vs new entries get different energy levels

## 2. Movement Language Conventions
Write out the standard phrases and framing for each movement type:
- New entries ("straight in at…", "a new entry at number…", "debuts at…")
- Climbers ("climbs X places", "up from number X", "rockets up to…")
- Fallers ("slips X places", "drops back", "down from its peak of…")
- Non-movers ("holds firm", "stays put", "a non-mover at…")
- Re-entries ("returns to the chart", "back in the Top 10 after X weeks away")
- #1 ("still the one to beat", "extends its run", "knocked off the top spot by…", "takes the crown")

## 3. Pacing and Energy
- Position 10-6: conversational, brief, set the scene
- Position 5-4: slightly more energy, acknowledge we're in the top half
- Position 3: first "big reveal" moment
- Position 2: tension — "so close but…"
- Position 1: the payoff — maximum energy, celebration or surprise
- How to vary energy week-to-week based on what the chart actually did

## 4. Narrative Framing
- Finding "the story" each week (dominant track, surprise entry, battle for #1)
- How to frame a static chart (no drama) vs a volatile chart (lots of movement)
- Recurring narratives (consecutive weeks at #1, "will it finally drop?", comeback stories)
- How many weeks on chart changes the framing of a track

## 5. Broadcast-Specific Conventions
- Back-announcing (what you say after a track plays)
- Segues between positions
- Time cues and pacing for a 1-2 hour show
- How DJs personalise within the format (ad-libs, reactions)
- The difference between scripted reads and spontaneous delivery

## 6. Chart Show Archetypes
Briefly reference the conventions established by:
- UK Official Chart (Radio 1) — fast-paced, youth-oriented
- Casey Kasem's American Top 40 — storytelling, artist dedications
- ARIA Chart Show — Australian broadcast style
- Note what's universal across all of them

Write this as a reference document an AI agent will use to inform its writing. Keep it practical — conventions and patterns, not history or opinion. Use examples of phrasing throughout. The document should be in markdown format.
```
